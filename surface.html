Why a cleared syndrome is not enough

Once HX(e ⊕ c) = 0, the remaining Z operator is either a stabilizer or a logical Z times a stabilizer. The syndrome cannot distinguish those two classes. Successful recovery requires CE ∈ 𝒮, where 𝒮 is the stabilizer group.

A nontrivial Z̄ changes the relative phase of an arbitrary encoded state: α|0L⟩ + β|1L⟩ → α|0L⟩ − β|1L⟩. It does not flip the logical computational-basis bit.

Geometry, boundaries, and model limits

This is an unrotated distance-5 planar CSS code. Its 41 data qubits lie on edges. The 20 measured X checks sit at non-boundary vertices; the 20 Z stabilizers surround faces. Boundary faces have weight 3. The left and right boundaries are rough, allowing Z chains to terminate without a measured endpoint.

The example uses only Pauli Z errors, perfect syndrome extraction, uniform unit-cost UF growth, and deterministic breadth-first peeling forests. It models one recovery round, without measurement errors, circuit faults, correlations, or time. Outcomes here are exact for the displayed error pattern, not a measured logical error rate.

Both check matrices have rank 20 and HXHZT = 0, leaving 41 − 20 − 20 = 1 logical qubit. The displayed Z̄ and X̄ overlap on one data qubit and anticommute.

Is the physical correction actually applied?

The display treats C as a Pauli operator for bookkeeping. A controller can instead track C in a Pauli frame and interpret later operations or measurements accordingly. The logical success criterion is still whether the tracked correction and actual error differ by a stabilizer.

In a real run, the decoder does not know E and cannot use this simulation’s logical verdict. Memory experiments use suitable logical preparation and final readout to assess outcomes; syndrome checks alone do not reveal an arbitrary encoded state.
